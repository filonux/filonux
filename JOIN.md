## Why now

Since the day we were born, we have all been living through the fastest period of technological and social change in human history. Things have become far more complex; technology has outgrown the average person's ability to understand it. This is a problem as serious as the gap between wealth and poverty, if not more so. Because when most people don't know how the things they use every day work, other groups with more power and money will take advantage of it.

We have already witnessed this today. Technocrats with more power than governments, tech megalomaniacs who are already directly inside the great White House (see the Musk case and how he directly influenced who would govern, even securing a position that gave a corporation disproportionate privileges over citizens).

Dystopia has become reality, and if you look to the future, its trends, the paths already laid out, what is coming will be far worse than anything we have seen. But all is not lost. The good news is that we have everything we need to face this situation. We have the knowledge, the software that shapes the world, the skills, the intentions and, above all, the will to change this trend.

"Everything changes fast" falls short of what we've lived through and what awaits us. We cannot accept privacy being violated or becoming a forgotten concept from a past culture, because everything starts there: that loss of control over what others know about you is what gives them the advantage to control the masses and steer them into dead ends so they can then impose their draconian measures. See the case of Spain, where even to practice something as innocent and simple as fishing, a person has to download an app to report the place where they fish, the time, photos of their catches, after having paid for a license in their own name just to be able to fish. This is only one example of how far they want to control every aspect and behavior of citizens, just because technology allows it. Add biometrics, the millions of cameras, artificial intelligence as a control assistant, and the result makes an episode of *Black Mirror* look like a joke.

The media are in the hands of a few owners (you can look into it: you'll find that a handful of corporations, 7 or 8, own even the most disgusting digital newspaper). Through them, over time, they have wrecked cognition and critical thinking, dumbing humans down, making them mediocre and turning them into a model to follow and imitate (for example, MrBeast, that famous YouTuber). What seems normal to us — that the lowest and most absurd side of human beings finds a worldwide audience that will follow it in all its nonsense — was impossible a few decades ago. All of this has had, and still has, effects that always degrade human beings to their most primitive state, turn their lives into an artifice, reduce them to a thing, and turn them into a product. Social media and the mass media lock people into echo chambers that dehumanize them and turn them into programmable NPCs, entrenched in a distortion of reality.

They have built systems of control and hypersurveillance with top-level biometrics. There are opaque databases (Europol) in democratic governments that spy on millions of citizens for no reason, with no control over who accesses all that data and without anyone having given them permission. And now they will use all the artificial intelligence and big data they constantly collect to mold any society in their own image and likeness, because they believe they are our gods.

All this centralization of power, technology, corruption, and wealth, together with the cognitive warfare that governments and tech corporations like Palantir wage around the clock against the population, will require committed, organized minds to work to counter it. And to keep open a possibility that would otherwise end up buried under algorithms, fads, or cultural hijackings turned into products to be marketed and worn out (Anonymous is one example), we have to step forward, have a vision, have a plan, and have real goals to pursue.

People are always asking how to change this or that, but they don't know what to do concretely. Well, I intend to use everything I've learned as a philosopher throughout my life to know what needs to change and how to do it, because this is not immutable, it is not a law of nature; we created this and we can change it. They are not the masters of humanity, and we can prove it to them.

These people are, for me, "Them": you can read the 22-point manifesto Palantir published in April 2026, a summary of the book by its CEO, Alex Karp, *The Technological Republic*. Amid so much megalomania, ethnocentrism, and ravings, we can see their message and goals clearly. Ours will be exactly the opposite. We will use exactly the same technology they do to confront them. Every action generates an equal and opposite reaction.

Palantir's software has been embedded for a decade in the domestic intelligence service (the DGSI): since 2016, with the contract renewed in December 2025. We already know what that means: technology that is installed in a society for decades, shapes it and controls it, while a few powerful technocrats decide for everyone what we will live and be. Once inside, their software, like a virus, has embedded itself and become normalized; it is part of all the infrastructure it sits in, and the enormous effort needed to readapt all the equipment, train people, secure everything again, etc., will be a good excuse for it to stay that way for a long time. In June 2026, the French government announced it will replace it with the French company ChapsVision, but gradually: in the meantime, Palantir's software will remain in use.

For all of this, we need forceful answers that point the way. We have the tools, the knowledge, and the technology to build a solution proportionate to the complex changes and challenges ahead.

## What we believe

1. Users must have privacy and security by design. They are allowed to own and choose what won't harm them later.
2. Local first: your data, your machine, your key. No telemetry, and nobody monitoring you.
3. Open source and decisions that are documented, cross-checked, and tested. Tools that give power back to the user.
4. Accessible: Linux shouldn't require being an expert.
5. Psychology: no dark patterns in design, and no promising what isn't there.
6. With few resources, incredible solutions can be built: ambition with its feet on the ground. With many resources, there are no limits.
7. Dealing with each other as equals: direct, respectful, no artificial hierarchies.
